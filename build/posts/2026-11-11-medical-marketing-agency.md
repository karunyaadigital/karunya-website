Many doctors start looking for help online by typing "marketing agency" and then quickly realise that most agencies work with restaurants, shops and real estate. A **medical marketing agency** is different. It works only, or mainly, with doctors, clinics and hospitals, and it has to know the rules that apply to them.

This guide explains what a medical marketing agency actually does, how it differs from a general agency, what it should not do under the 2026 rules, and how to choose one. It is written for doctors, clinic owners and hospital admins who are about to hire.

## What a medical marketing agency does

The work is less about "advertising" and more about helping patients find you and understand your field. In practice, that usually means:

- **Google Business Profile:** correct name, timings, address, categories, photos of the clinic, and polite, factual review replies.
- **Patient education content:** Reels, posts and short videos that explain conditions, tests and treatments in simple language.
- **Website and SEO:** clear pages for each service, doctor profiles, location details and helpful articles so you show up when people search.
- **Doctor branding:** a consistent look, bio and tone across Google, Instagram, YouTube and the website.
- **Compliance checking:** reading each post against the National Medical Commission (NMC) rules before it goes live.
- **Reporting:** monthly numbers you can verify yourself, such as profile views, calls and website visits.

A good agency writes the scripts, guides the shoot and handles editing and posting. You stay the expert on camera and the final approver.

## Medical marketing agency vs general digital agency

| Point | General digital agency | Medical marketing agency |
|---|---|---|
| Rules knowledge | May not know NMC or NDC rules | Should check every post against them |
| Typical tactics | Offers, discounts, testimonials, "best in city" | Factual, educational content |
| Content | Brand ads and trends | Explains conditions, tests and care |
| Approval | Often fast, light review | Doctor approves every post |
| Reviews | May push for more reviews | Replies only; never buys or solicits |
| Payment | Sometimes pay-per-lead | Fixed fee, not linked to patients |

The second column is not "bad marketing". It just does not fit healthcare any more. Tactics that work for a café can put a doctor's registration at risk.

## What the NMC 2026 rules mean for any agency you hire

The NMC *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* came on 6 October 2026. According to a Healthus.ai summary of the guidelines:

- Publishing through a third party does not remove the doctor's or hospital's responsibility. Outsourcing does not shift it.
- Agency fees must not be linked to procuring or referring individual patients.
- A doctor may not use an intermediary to do what they cannot do themselves.
- Doctors may not request or share patient testimonials or reviews on social media for promotion, and patient consent does not change this.
- Fake, paid or gated reviews and manipulating search rankings are prohibited.
- AI may not create or alter a patient's image, voice, testimonial or clinical outcome.

So a medical marketing agency is only useful if it works inside these rules. If it offers testimonial videos, before/after photos, "No.1 doctor" banners, review buying or pay-per-patient deals, it is offering risk. ## Questions to ask before you hire

Use these in your first call with any agency:

1. **How many doctors or clinics do you work with now?** You want healthcare experience, not a one-off.
2. **Who checks each post against the NMC rules?** There should be a named person and a clear process.
3. **Will I approve every post before it goes live?** The answer should be yes.
4. **How are you paid?** A fixed monthly fee or project fee is safe. Payment per patient or per lead is not.
5. **Who owns my accounts?** Your Google profile, Instagram and website should stay in your name.
6. **Who shoots the videos?** Some agencies send a crew; others give scripts and guidance so you record on your phone.
7. **What will the monthly report show?** Ask for numbers you can check inside Google or Instagram yourself.

## Red flags

| What you hear | Why it is a problem |
|---|---|
| "We'll make you the No.1 doctor in the city" | Superlative claims are restricted |
| "Let's film happy patient stories" | Patient testimonials are not allowed for promotion |
| "We'll get you 100 reviews this month" | Buying or soliciting reviews is prohibited |
| "Pay us only for each patient we send" | Fees must not be linked to patients |
| "AI can make a patient video for you" | AI cannot create patient images or outcomes |
| "We'll keep the Instagram login" | You lose control of your own account |

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a medical marketing agency?**
It is an agency that works mainly with doctors, clinics and hospitals. It manages Google profiles, patient education content, websites and SEO, and checks content against medical advertising rules.

**Is a medical marketing agency better than a general agency for doctors?**
Not automatically. What matters is whether the agency knows the NMC rules, lets you approve every post and is paid a fixed fee rather than per patient. Healthcare experience makes this more likely.

**Can an agency post patient testimonials for me if the patient agrees?**
No. Summaries of the NMC 2026 guidelines say doctors may not request or share patient testimonials for promotion, and consent does not change this.

**If the agency breaks the rules, am I responsible?**
Yes. Summaries of the guidelines say outsourcing does not shift the doctor's or hospital's responsibility, so approve every post yourself.

**Do I need to hire a video crew?**
Not always. Many doctors record on a phone using a script and guidance from the agency, which keeps costs lower.
