## What a digital marketing agency for doctors actually does

A good agency helps patients find you and understand your work. It does not "sell" you like a product. In practice, the work usually falls into five parts.

| Service | What it includes | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Correct category, timings, map pin, photos, review replies | Most local searches start here |
| Website and SEO | Service pages, doctor profile, location pages, page speed | Helps you appear for searches like "skin doctor near me" |
| Instagram and Reels | Topic ideas, scripts, shoot guidance, editing, posting | Patient education in a format people actually watch |
| YouTube | Longer explainers on common questions | Builds trust before the first visit |
| Compliance review | Every post checked against NMC rules | Protects your registration |

## Why the choice of agency matters more in 2026

The NMC's *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* (6 October 2026) changed the ground rules. One point is especially important when you hire anyone.

According to a summary of the guidelines by HMS Consultants, using an agency or influencer does not shift responsibility away from the doctor. You remain answerable for content you authorised, commissioned or knowingly allowed.

So the agency is not just a vendor. It is effectively writing in your name. If it posts a testimonial or a "best doctor" claim, the consequences can land on you. Our post on [NMC penalties for doctors' advertising](blog-nmc-penalties-for-doctors-advertising.html) explains the graded penalties, from a warning up to suspension.

## How agencies charge doctors

Pricing in this market is not standard, and we won't guess at other agencies' prices. What you can compare is the **model** of charging.

| Pricing model | How it works | Is it safe under NMC rules? |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed monthly retainer | Set fee for a defined list of work | Yes, the cleanest option |
| Project fee | One-time fee, e.g. a website build | Yes |
| Ad spend + management fee | You pay the platform, agency charges to manage | Only if the ads themselves follow the rules |
| Pay per patient / per lead | Agency is paid for each patient or enquiry | Avoid: reported as barred by the guidelines |

The HMS Consultants summary says agency, influencer and platform contracts must not pay per patient procured or referred, and that lead-generation fees tied to referrals are also barred. If an agency's main pitch is "you only pay per patient", walk away.

## Red flags to watch for

Under the 2026 rules, an agency that offers any of the following is putting your registration at risk:

- Patient testimonials or "patient story" videos
- Before/after photos of treatment
- Words like "best", "No.1", "guaranteed" or "100% success" in your posts or ads
- Buying reviews, followers or views, or asking patients for reviews in return for anything
- Discounts, "free consultation" offers or ads soliciting patients for an individual doctor
- AI-made patient stories or results (the summary also notes permitted AI content must be labelled)
- Payment linked to the number of patients or leads

If you see these in an agency's own sample work, that tells you how they will handle your account too.

## Seven questions to ask before you sign

1. **Who checks every post against NMC rules?** Ask for a named person and the process.
2. **Will I approve each post before it goes live?** The answer should be yes.
3. **What exactly do I get each month?** Ask for numbers: reels, posts, articles, profile updates.
4. **How do you charge?** A fixed fee for defined work is the safe answer.
5. **Who owns the accounts and content?** Your Google profile, Instagram and videos should stay in your name.
6. **What will the monthly report show?** Look for figures you can check yourself, like profile views, calls and direction requests.
7. **Do you keep a record of who wrote and approved each post?** The HMS summary recommends keeping such a log.

## Agency, freelancer or doing it yourself

| Option | Good fit when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Do it yourself | You enjoy creating content and have weekly time | Consistency drops when the OPD gets busy |
| Freelancer | You need one skill, like editing | May not know NMC rules; no backup if they leave |
| Specialised agency | You want Google, Instagram and website handled together | Check compliance process and contract terms |

A general agency that mostly serves restaurants or shops can still do good work. Just check that it understands medical advertising rules before you hand over your accounts.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can doctors legally hire a digital marketing agency in India?**
Yes. Doctors can share factual, educational information and get help doing it. The doctor stays responsible for what is published, so the agency must follow the NMC guidelines.

**How much does a digital marketing agency for doctors cost?**
It varies by agency and scope. Compare what is included, not just the fee. Karunya Digital's plans are Rs 20,000, Rs 25,000 and Rs 40,000 per month.

**Is pay-per-lead marketing allowed for doctors?**
Reported summaries of the guidelines say payment linked to patients procured or referred is barred. A fixed monthly fee is the safer model.

**How long does it take to see results?**
Profile fixes can show up in local searches fairly soon. Content and SEO build over months. Be wary of anyone who guarantees fast results.

**Do I need to appear on camera?**
It helps, because patients want to hear from their doctor. But it can be simple: a phone, a quiet room and a script.
