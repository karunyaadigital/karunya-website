Most lists of **dental clinic marketing ideas** online were written for a different time. They tell you to post smile makeovers, share happy patient videos and run "free check-up" offers. In October 2026, the rules for dentists in India changed, and many of those ideas now put your registration at risk.

This guide gives you practical ideas that still work, and explains which old ideas to drop.

## What changed for dentists in October 2026

On 14 October 2026, the Ethics and Dental Registration Board of the **National Dental Commission** issued an advisory to all registered dentists about social media and advertising. It took effect immediately.

As reported by Medical Dialogues, the advisory highlights these as unethical:

- Recording or broadcasting consultations and dental procedures
- Publishing patient testimonials and before-and-after photos
- Misleading treatment claims
- Sharing inaccurate health information
- Self-praising publicity
- Signboards that are too large

The advisory points to the Revised Dentists (Code of Ethics) Regulations, 2014. Established violations can lead to a show-cause notice, or suspension or removal from the State and National Register of Dentists.

Around the same time, the NMC released its own *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* (6 October 2026) for medical doctors. The spirit is the same: inform patients, don't sell to them. If you also work with MBBS colleagues or run a multi-speciality set-up, read our [NMC new guidelines explainer](blog-nmc-new-guidelines-doctors-social-media.html).

## Old ideas vs safe ideas: a quick table

| Old idea | Status now | Safe alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Before-and-after smile photos | Flagged in the advisory | Diagrams or models explaining a procedure |
| Patient video testimonials | Flagged in the advisory | Dentist explains common questions on camera |
| Filming a live procedure | Flagged in the advisory | Explain steps using a typodont or animation |
| "Best dentist in the city" | Self-praise | Factual: qualifications, services, timings |
| "Free check-up camp" ads | Solicitation risk | General oral-health awareness posts |
| Huge glowing signboard | Flagged in the advisory | Clear, proportionate board with name and degree |

## Idea 1: Get your Google Business Profile right

When someone searches "dentist near me", Google shows the map results first. Your profile is often the first thing a patient sees.

- Use your real clinic name, exactly as on your board
- Choose the correct category (for example, "Dentist" or "Dental clinic")
- Keep timings, phone number and map pin accurate, including holidays
- Add real photos of the clinic exterior, reception and equipment, with no patients in them
- List services in plain words: root canal treatment, scaling, braces, implants

This is information, not promotion, and it helps patients reach you. Our [Google Business Profile for doctors checklist](blog-google-business-profile-for-doctors.html) walks through each field.

## Idea 2: Teach, don't sell, on Instagram and Reels

Patients have many simple questions they feel shy to ask in the chair. Each one is a short Reel:

- How often should I change my toothbrush?
- Why do my teeth feel sensitive to cold?
- Is a root canal painful? What actually happens?
- When should my child first see a dentist?
- What is the difference between scaling and whitening?

Keep it accurate, calm and general. Don't promise results, and don't show a real patient's mouth. A dentist speaking to camera in a clean clinic, with a model or a diagram, builds more trust than any offer post.

If you're setting up your profile, our guide on the [bio for doctors on Instagram](blog-bio-for-doctors-on-instagram.html) has templates you can adapt.

## Idea 3: A simple, honest website

A one-page "Dr Name, BDS" site is not enough any more. A good dental website has:

- One page per service, explaining what it is, who may need it and what to expect
- Clear clinic address, timings and a tap-to-call button
- Dentist qualifications and registration details
- An FAQ section that answers real patient questions

Each service page can rank on Google for searches like "root canal treatment in Varanasi". Our [SEO for doctors guide](blog-seo-for-doctors.html) explains how.

## Idea 4: Make the clinic easy to come back to

Good marketing is also what happens after the patient walks in.

- Send appointment reminders on WhatsApp to existing patients who agreed to receive them
- Give a printed aftercare sheet after extractions or scaling
- Keep waiting areas clean, with a clear fee board if you have one

None of this needs a discount or an offer. It simply makes the clinic easier to trust.

## Idea 5: Awareness days and school-age oral care

Oral-health awareness days and themes like children's brushing habits, tobacco and gum health, or diabetes and gums make useful education posts. Keep them about the topic, not about your clinic's prices.

## What not to do, even if competitors do

- Don't post smile transformations, even with patient consent
- Don't share Google reviews as testimonials on Instagram (see our [Google reviews under NMC rules guide](blog-google-reviews-for-doctors-nmc.html))
- Don't run "50% off braces" or "free consultation" ads
- Don't use AI to create fake patient stories
- Don't pay any agency or app per patient brought in

## Frequently asked questions

**Can dentists post on Instagram at all after the 2026 advisory?**
Yes. The advisory targets unethical advertising, like testimonials, before-and-after photos and misleading claims. Accurate, educational content about oral health is still a good use of social media.

**Can I show before-and-after photos if the patient agrees?**
It's best not to. The National Dental Commission advisory specifically flags before-and-after photographs, and consent doesn't change that.

**Is a Google Business Profile allowed for a dental clinic?**
Yes. Keeping your clinic's name, address, timings and services correct on Google is factual patient information.

**What happens if a dentist breaks these rules?**
According to reports on the advisory, established violations can lead to a show-cause notice, or suspension or removal from the State and National Register of Dentists.

**Can an agency handle all this for my dental clinic?**
Yes, but you stay responsible for what goes out in your name. Pick an agency that writes educational content and says no to testimonials and offers.
