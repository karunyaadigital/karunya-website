Periods, PCOS, pregnancy, menopause. Many women look up these topics online before they visit a doctor, and many feel shy asking in person. That is why more gynaecologists and women's clinics now search for a **gynaecologist marketing agency** to help them share correct, simple information.

Gynaecology also carries more rules than most specialities. Along with the NMC's 2026 advertising guidelines, the PCPNDT Act bans any advertising linked to sex determination. This guide explains what an agency should do for a gynaecology practice, what it must never do, and how to choose one.

## What a gynaecologist marketing agency should do

The goal is patient education and being easy to find, not "selling" deliveries or procedures. A sensible scope includes:

- **Education Reels and posts:** periods, PCOS, anaemia in pregnancy, antenatal check-ups, menopause, cervical cancer screening, in general terms.
- **Scripts and shoot guidance:** simple Hindi, Hinglish or English scripts that the doctor records on a phone.
- **Editing, captions and posting:** subtitles, clean thumbnails and a steady schedule.
- **Google Business Profile:** correct timings, address, phone, services, doctor details and clinic photos.
- **Website pages:** a page for each condition or service you offer, your qualifications and how to reach you.
- **Compliance check:** every post checked against NMC and PCPNDT rules before your approval.
- **Monthly reporting:** calls, direction requests, profile views, website visits and content reach.

## Why gynaecology needs extra care

Women's health topics are personal. Patients notice when a page feels respectful and when it feels like an advertisement. Content should use correct medical words, avoid fear, and never show or hint at a real patient.

Privacy matters even more here. A Reel filmed in the waiting room, a photo of a baby with the mother's name, or a reply to a comment that reveals someone's pregnancy can all break patient confidentiality. A good agency builds these checks into its process.

## The rules that matter for gynaecology marketing

**NMC 2026 guidelines.** The NMC's *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* came into force on 6 October 2026. As reported by The South First, they bar soliciting or buying reviews, sharing patient testimonials for promotion, "guaranteed" outcome claims, claims of being better than other doctors, and patient photos, videos or before/after content, even with consent. AI-made promotional content counts as advertising. Posts must show the doctor's name, qualifications, registration status and register number. Using an agency does not protect the doctor if the doctor authorised or knowingly allowed the content.

**PCPNDT Act.** Section 22 of the Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques Act prohibits advertisements related to pre-conception or pre-natal sex determination, as Drishti IAS explains. Offences under the Act are cognizable, non-bailable and non-compoundable. So any post, ad, caption or reply that even hints at "boy or girl" tests or sex prediction must never go out.

## What your agency must never suggest

| What some agencies offer | Why to refuse |
|---|---|
| Mother-and-baby "happy delivery" videos | Works as a patient testimonial and risks privacy |
| "Painless normal delivery guaranteed" | Guaranteed outcome claims are barred |
| "Best lady doctor in the city" | Superlative claims are not allowed |
| Free pregnancy check-up camp or discount packages | Free-consultation and discount offers are a problem |
| Any hint at baby's gender, "boy-or-girl" myths content | Sex-determination advertising is banned under the PCPNDT Act |
| Paid ads asking women to book a delivery or surgery | Soliciting patients and promoting procedures is barred |
| AI-made patient stories or "real experience" voiceovers | Synthetic patient experiences are barred |
| Asking patients for 5-star reviews, or buying them | Soliciting or buying reviews is barred |
| Pay per lead or per delivery | Fees must not be linked to patient referrals |

## Good content ideas for gynaecologists

These educate without promoting a procedure:

- What is normal and not normal about periods.
- PCOS in simple words: signs, and why a doctor's check matters.
- Why antenatal check-ups are scheduled the way they are.
- Common pregnancy myths about food and rest.
- Menopause: what changes, and when to see a doctor.
- Why cervical cancer screening matters, in general terms.

## Questions to ask before hiring

1. Have you worked with gynaecologists or women's clinics? Can I see their public profiles?
2. Who checks each post against NMC and PCPNDT rules?
3. Will every script, post and comment reply come to me for approval?
4. How do you keep patients, babies and the waiting room out of all content?
5. Do you send a crew, or guide my team to record on a phone?
6. Will all accounts stay in my name?
7. Is the fee a fixed monthly amount, not linked to enquiries or deliveries?
8. How will you handle comments asking for personal medical advice?

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a gynaecologist post about normal delivery or C-section?**
Yes, as general education: what each involves, why doctors choose one over the other, and what recovery usually looks like. Avoid promoting the procedure or promising outcomes.

**Can I post photos of newborn babies from my clinic?**
It is safer not to. Patient photos for promotion are restricted under the NMC rules, and newborn photos can reveal the family's identity.

**Can my agency make content on "boy or girl" myths?**
Stay away from this topic. Any content linked to sex determination risks breaking the PCPNDT Act, even if the intent is to bust myths.

**Is a Google Business Profile useful for a gynae clinic?**
Yes. Pregnant women and their families often search nearby and call, so correct timings, phone number, emergency availability details and directions matter.

**Should a lady doctor appear on camera herself?**
It helps patients trust the information, but it is your choice. Voiceovers with simple graphics also work for education content.
