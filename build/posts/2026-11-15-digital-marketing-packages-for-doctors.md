Most agencies show their work as packages: a small plan, a middle plan and a big plan. That looks simple, but the names hide a lot. One agency's "basic" can include more real work than another agency's "premium". So before you sign, you need to know what **digital marketing packages for doctors** should actually contain.

This guide explains the usual package levels, what each level should include, the questions to ask, and what the NMC's 2026 rules say a package must never include. It is written for doctors, clinic owners and hospital admins who are comparing agencies.

## How digital marketing packages for doctors are usually built

A package is a bundle of monthly tasks. Most agencies in India mix the same building blocks in different amounts:

- **Google Business Profile:** correct timings, services, photos of the clinic, regular updates and replies to reviews.
- **Instagram content:** Reels, posts and stories that educate patients.
- **Scripts and shoot guidance:** what you will say on camera and how to record it.
- **Editing and posting:** captions, subtitles, scheduling.
- **Website and SEO:** pages for the conditions you treat, so patients can find you on Google.
- **YouTube:** Shorts or longer explainers.
- **Strategy and reporting:** a monthly plan and a report you can understand.

The price changes with how many blocks are included and how much of each. A plan with 4 Reels and one with 12 Reels are very different jobs, even if both are called "social media management".

## What each package level should include

There is no official standard, so treat this as a fair checklist rather than a rule.

| Package level | Who it suits | What it should include | What to confirm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | A single doctor starting online | Google Business Profile care, a few Reels with scripts and editing, a content calendar, a monthly report | Exact number of Reels, who checks NMC compliance |
| Growth | A doctor who wants steady visibility in the city | Everything in Starter, more Reels, review replies, a monthly strategy call, profile and bio work | How often you meet, who writes review replies |
| Premium / Authority | A senior doctor or busy speciality clinic | Everything in Growth, higher-end editing, YouTube Shorts, competitor and keyword research | What "high-end" means, sample work |
| Hospital / custom | Multi-speciality hospitals | Separate plans per department and doctor, approval process for many doctors | Who approves each doctor's content |

If a package lists only words like "branding", "engagement" or "360° marketing" without numbers, ask for numbers in writing.

## Questions to ask before you pick a package

1. How many Reels, posts, stories and blog articles are included each month?
2. Who writes the scripts, and who checks them against the NMC rules?
3. Who shoots the video? Do you guide me to record on my phone, or send a crew at extra cost?
4. Is Google Business Profile included, or is it a separate charge?
5. Is ad spend, if any, inside the fee or extra? (Read the NMC section below first.)
6. Will every post come to me for approval before it goes live?
7. Will all accounts stay in my name, with my email and phone number?
8. What is the notice period, and how do I get my files if we stop?

## What no package should include under the NMC 2026 rules

The National Medical Commission's *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* came into force on 6 October 2026. As reported by The South First, doctors may share health awareness and educational content, but may not use it to promote their own practice or solicit patients. The same report says using an agency or influencer does not shield a doctor: if you approved or knowingly allowed a post, you are responsible.

So a package that lists any of these is a warning sign:

- Patient testimonial videos or "happy patient" stories, even with consent.
- Before/after photos or videos.
- Captions such as "best doctor", "No.1", "guaranteed" or "100% result".
- Buying followers, likes, reviews or ratings, or asking patients for reviews in return for anything.
- Discount posts or "free consultation" offers.
- Paid ads that ask patients to come to an individual doctor's clinic.
- AI-made patient faces, voices or stories.
- Pay-per-patient or pay-per-lead pricing.

A good package is built around patient education and being easy to find: correct Google profile details, clear explainers and accurate information about you and your clinic. ## Fixed monthly fee vs other pricing models

| Pricing model | How it works | Fit for doctors in 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed monthly retainer | Same fee each month for agreed work | Clear and simple; easy to compare |
| Per-task pricing | You pay per Reel or per post | Fine for small needs; can add up |
| One-time setup + monthly | Setup fee for profile/website, then a retainer | Ask what the setup actually covers |
| Pay per lead / per patient | Fee linked to patients or enquiries | Avoid: fees must not be linked to patient referrals |

## How to compare two packages fairly

Put both quotes side by side and write down, for each one, the number of Reels, posts and stories, whether Google Business Profile is included, who shoots, who checks compliance, how often you get a report, and the notice period. Then divide the fee by the real work. This "price per real task" view is far more useful than the package name.

Also ask to see public profiles of doctors the agency already manages. That shows the actual quality of scripts and editing better than any sales deck.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which package should a new clinic start with?**
Usually the smallest plan that covers Google Business Profile and a steady number of educational Reels. You can move up once you see what you can keep up with.

**Is ad spend included in digital marketing packages for doctors?**
It depends on the agency, so ask. Remember that ads soliciting patients for an individual doctor are a problem under the NMC 2026 guidelines.

**Can I change my package later?**
Most agencies allow it with notice. Ask for the upgrade and downgrade terms in writing before you start.

**Should a package include getting more Google reviews?**
It can include replying to reviews politely. It should not include buying reviews or offering patients anything for a review.

**Do packages differ for hospitals?**
Yes. Hospitals need content for many doctors and departments, and an approval process for each doctor, so they usually need a custom plan.
