Couples who are thinking about IVF usually research for weeks before they visit a clinic. They read, watch videos and compare centres quietly, often without telling family. That makes online information very important for a fertility centre, and it is why many IVF specialists search for an **IVF clinic marketing agency**.

But IVF is also one of the most sensitive areas in healthcare marketing. Patients are emotional, results vary, and there are extra laws on top of the NMC's 2026 advertising rules. This guide explains what a good agency should do for an IVF clinic, which limits apply, what to ask, and which offers to refuse.

## What an IVF clinic marketing agency should actually do

The goal is not to "sell IVF". The goal is to help couples understand their options and find correct information about your centre and doctors. Good agency work for a fertility clinic usually includes:

- **Patient education content:** simple explainers on infertility tests, IUI vs IVF, what happens at each step, timelines and common myths.
- **Google Business Profile care:** correct timings, address, services, doctor names and clinic photos, so couples can find and reach you.
- **Website pages:** clear pages on treatments you offer, your team and their qualifications, and what a first visit involves.
- **Scripts and shoot guidance:** short Reel scripts in Hindi, Hinglish or English that the doctor can record on a phone.
- **Editing and posting:** subtitles, captions and a steady schedule.
- **Privacy-first approach:** no patient faces, names or identifiable stories, ever.
- **Monthly reporting:** profile views, calls, direction requests, website visits and content reach.

## Rules that apply to IVF clinic marketing

Two sets of rules matter most.

**NMC 2026 guidelines.** The National Medical Commission's *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* came into force on 6 October 2026. As reported by The South First, doctors may take part in health awareness and educational content but may not use it to promote their practice or solicit patients, and using an agency does not shield the doctor from responsibility for what is posted.

**ART (Regulation) Act, 2021.** As summarised in the National Medical Journal of India, ART clinics, banks and their agents are prohibited from issuing or publishing any advertisement for sex-selective ART facilities in any medium, including online, and the Act provides heavy penalties for offering or promoting sex selection. A report on the ART framework (Usthadian, October 2026) also notes that ART clinics and banks must be registered with the National Registry of ART Clinics and Banks of India.

Your agency is one of those "agents", so it must understand these rules well.

## What your agency must never suggest

| What some agencies offer | Why to refuse |
|---|---|
| "Success rate" or "guaranteed pregnancy" claims | Guaranteed and misleading claims are barred; results vary per couple |
| Baby photos or "happy parents" testimonial videos | Patient testimonials are not allowed, even with consent |
| "Best IVF centre" or "No.1 fertility clinic" | Superlative claims are not allowed |
| Discount packages or "free consultation" camps | Discount and free-consultation offers are a problem under NMC rules |
| Any mention of choosing the baby's sex | Banned under the ART Act, with serious penalties |
| Paid ads asking couples to come to the clinic | Soliciting patients for an individual doctor is a problem |
| AI-made couples, babies or stories | Promotional AI content counts as advertising |
| Pay per enquiry or pay per IVF cycle | Agency fees must not be linked to patient referrals |
| Buying reviews or ratings | Manipulating reviews and rankings is barred |

## What good IVF content looks like

Good content answers the questions couples are too shy to ask in person. Some safe topics:

- What tests a couple usually needs before treatment.
- The difference between IUI, IVF and ICSI, in plain words.
- What to expect on the day of egg collection or embryo transfer.
- Common myths about infertility, explained calmly.
- Lifestyle questions, with honest "it depends" answers.

Each piece should be medically checked by the doctor, approved before posting, and free of promises. Our [healthcare marketing agency guide](blog-healthcare-marketing-agency.html) explains how this approval process should work.

## Questions to ask before hiring an IVF clinic marketing agency

1. Have you worked with fertility or gynaecology clinics before? Can I see their public profiles?
2. Who checks every post against the NMC guidelines and the ART Act?
3. How do you protect patient privacy in content and in comments?
4. Will every post come to me for approval first?
5. Who shoots the videos: do you guide our doctors to record on a phone, or send a crew?
6. Will all accounts stay in the clinic's name?
7. Is the fee a fixed monthly amount, not linked to enquiries or cycles?
8. How do you handle sensitive comments and messages from couples?

## Frequently asked questions

**Can an IVF clinic share its success rate online?**
Be very careful. Results vary per couple, and claims that promise or imply guaranteed results are barred. Educational information about how outcomes depend on age and other factors is safer, checked by the doctor.

**Can we post photos of babies born after treatment?**
No. That works as a patient testimonial, which the NMC 2026 guidelines do not allow, and it risks patient privacy.

**Should an IVF clinic run paid ads?**
Ads that solicit patients for an individual doctor are a problem under the NMC guidelines. Focus on education content, a complete Google profile and a clear website.

**Can the agency reply to couples' messages?**
It can send polite, general replies and share clinic contact details. Medical advice should come only from the doctor, privately.

**Does a gynaecology clinic need a different agency from an IVF centre?**
Not necessarily, but the agency must understand both NMC rules and the ART Act if IVF services are offered.
