You run a clinic. Patients still come through word of mouth, but you know more of them now search on Google and Instagram first. So you start calling agencies, and every one of them sounds confident. If you are wondering **how to choose a digital marketing agency for clinic** work, this guide gives you a simple checklist you can use in any city.

It covers what the agency should actually do, what the NMC's 2026 rules now allow, the questions to ask on the first call, and the red flags that should end the conversation.

## Start with what your clinic needs

Before you compare agencies, write down three things on one page:

- **Your goal.** For example: "Patients nearby should find correct timings and directions", or "I want to explain common conditions in my speciality in simple Hindi."
- **Your time.** How many minutes a week can you give for recording videos and approving posts?
- **Your accounts.** Who owns your Google Business Profile, Instagram, Facebook page and website today? Do you have the logins?

This page stops you from buying services you don't need. A small clinic usually needs a complete Google profile, a clear website and steady education content. It rarely needs a big ad budget.

## What a good clinic agency should do

The work should help patients understand health topics and find correct information about your clinic. A typical scope looks like this:

| Work area | What it means | Your role |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Timings, address, phone, services, photos, doctor details | Approve changes, keep account ownership |
| Patient education content | Reels and posts on common questions in your speciality | Check medical facts, approve |
| Scripts and shoot guidance | Simple scripts you record on a phone | Record when convenient |
| Editing and posting | Subtitles, thumbnails, steady schedule | None |
| Website | Service pages, doctor profile, how to reach you | Approve content |
| Compliance check | Every post checked against NMC rules | Final sign-off |
| Monthly report | Calls, direction requests, profile views, reach | Review, ask questions |

If an agency's proposal has no compliance step and no approval step, that is already a warning.

## The NMC 2026 rules every clinic agency must know

The NMC's *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* came into force on 6 October 2026. As reported by The South First, they say doctors and hospitals must not:

- Solicit, buy or manipulate patient reviews, ratings or testimonials.
- Request or share patient testimonials for promotion on social media.
- Make "guaranteed" outcome claims or claims of being superior to other doctors.
- Promote in ways that could lead patients to unnecessary tests or procedures.
- Use patient photos, videos or before/after content (patient consent does not make it allowed).

AI-made content counts as advertising when it is promotional. Posts must show the doctor's name, qualifications, registration status and register number. Health education is allowed, as long as it does not promote your practice or solicit patients.

One line matters most when you hire an agency: **using an agency does not protect you.** If you authorised, commissioned or knowingly allowed the content, you are responsible. So the agency you choose must know these rules as well as you do. Read our [NMC new guidelines explainer](blog-nmc-new-guidelines-doctors-social-media.html) for more detail.

## 10 questions to ask on the first call

1. Which doctors or clinics do you work with now? Can I see their public profiles?
2. Who in your team checks every post against the NMC 2026 rules?
3. Will every script, post and reply template come to me for approval before it goes live?
4. Will my Google, Instagram and website accounts stay in my name, with me as owner?
5. What exactly is included each month, in writing?
6. Is the fee a fixed monthly amount, and not linked to the number of patients or enquiries?
7. Do you send a camera crew, or guide my team to record on a phone? (Many agencies, including us, only give scripts and shoot guidance.)
8. How do you handle comments asking for medical advice, and negative reviews?
9. What will the monthly report show?
10. What is the notice period, and what happens to my content and logins if I leave?

Good agencies answer these calmly and in writing. Our [freelancer vs agency guide](blog-freelancer-vs-agency-for-doctors.html) explains how the answers differ by who you hire.

## Red flags: walk away if you hear these

| What you hear | Why it is a problem |
|---|---|
| "We will get you 100 new patients a month" | Guaranteed claims are not realistic and the pitch often leads to banned tactics |
| "We'll post patient video reviews" | Patient testimonials are not allowed for promotion |
| "Before/after photos work best" | Before/after content is restricted under the rules |
| "We can buy 5-star reviews" or "we'll ask every patient for a review script" | Buying, soliciting or manipulating reviews is barred |
| "Run a free check-up camp ad" or "flat discount offer" | Free-consultation and discount offers are a problem |
| "Pay us per lead or per patient" | Fees linked to patient referrals are a problem |
| "We'll make you the No.1 doctor in your city" | Superlative claims are not allowed |
| "We'll keep the page under our account" | You can lose your own profile and history |

## How to compare two or three quotes

Ask each shortlisted agency for a one-page written scope. Then compare them side by side on the same points: what is included each month, how many posts, who writes scripts, the compliance process, account ownership, reporting and notice period.

Don't compare on price alone. A cheaper package with no compliance check can cost you far more if a post breaks the rules. Our [agency cost guide](blog-digital-marketing-agency-cost-for-doctors.html) explains what drives the monthly fee, so you can read any quote with clear eyes.

Finally, start with a short trial period if possible, like three months, and judge on steady work and clear communication, not on promises.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should a small clinic hire an agency at all?**
Not always. If you have time and only need a complete Google profile, you can do it yourself. An agency helps when you want steady education content, a proper website and a compliance check without spending your evenings on it.

**Is a local agency better than one in another city?**
Not by default. Knowing your city's language and patients helps, but the bigger checks are NMC knowledge, written scope, approvals and account ownership. Remote agencies can work well with phone recording and clear approvals.

**How long before I see results?**
Profile fixes can improve calls and direction requests fairly quickly, while content usually takes months of steady posting. Be careful of anyone who promises fast, guaranteed numbers.

**Can my agency reply to Google reviews for me?**
It can draft polite, general replies for your approval. Replies must never reveal any patient detail, and the agency must not buy or script reviews.

**What should be in the agency contract?**
Monthly scope, approval process, account ownership, a fixed fee not linked to patient numbers, reporting, notice period and handover of logins and content when you leave.
