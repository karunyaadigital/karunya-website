Search for a **healthcare marketing agency in India** and you will see dozens of names. Big agencies in metro cities, small teams in Tier 2 cities, freelancers, and listing sites with "top 10" pages. Almost all of them promise more patients. So how does a doctor, clinic owner or hospital admin actually compare them?

This guide gives you a simple way to compare agencies across India. It covers what the work should include, what the NMC's 2026 rules now allow, the questions to ask, and the red flags that should make you walk away.

## Why choosing a healthcare marketing agency in India changed in 2026

On 6 October 2026, the National Medical Commission issued its *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication*, effective immediately. As reported by MedBound Times, the guidelines cover websites, apps, social media, digital marketing, influencer campaigns, and search and platform promotion when content is promotional.

That means many tactics agencies used for years are now a risk for the doctor. As MedBound Times summarises, the guidelines bar:

- Patient testimonials and reviews used for professional promotion.
- Buying, soliciting or manipulating reviews, ratings or endorsements.
- Manipulating search rankings or engagement to create false narratives.
- "Best doctor" claims, and doctors are advised against "number one" claims too.
- Using social media to solicit patients or promote procedures for profit.
- AI-made testimonials or synthetic patient stories. Other AI content must be clearly labelled.

Penalties for doctors range from warnings and ethics training up to suspension of registration in serious or repeated cases. So the most important question when comparing agencies is no longer "who gets the most leads?" It is "who can help patients find and trust me without breaking the rules?"

## What a good healthcare marketing agency should do

Look for an agency whose work is about patient education and being found. A typical scope:

- **Google Business Profile:** correct address, timings, services, doctor names and clinic photos.
- **Patient education content:** Reels and posts that explain conditions, tests and treatments in simple language.
- **Scripts and shoot guidance:** so the doctor can record short videos on a phone.
- **Editing, captions and posting:** a steady, predictable schedule.
- **Website and SEO:** clear pages on what you treat, your qualifications and how to reach you.
- **Compliance checks:** every post checked against NMC rules and approved by the doctor.
- **Monthly reporting:** calls, direction requests, profile views, website visits and content reach.

The guidelines also say practitioner posts should show the doctor's name, qualifications, registration status and register number, according to MedBound Times. A good agency builds this into every profile and page.

## How to compare agencies: a simple scorecard

Use this table when you speak to two or three agencies. Give each row a tick or a cross.

| What to check | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Healthcare focus | Works mainly with doctors and hospitals | "We do every industry" |
| NMC knowledge | Can explain the October 2026 rules clearly | Says "nobody checks these rules" |
| Approval process | Every post comes to you before going live | Posts without asking |
| Account ownership | Profiles and ad accounts stay in your name | Agency owns your page or Google profile |
| Fee model | Fixed monthly fee with a written scope | Pay per lead or pay per patient |
| Content style | Education, facts, clinic information | Testimonials, before/after, "best doctor" |
| Reporting | Clear monthly numbers you can verify | Only "reach" and vanity numbers |
| Language | Can write in the language your patients use | One-size-fits-all English |

## Questions to ask before you sign

1. Which doctors or hospitals do you work with now? Can I see their public profiles?
2. Who checks content against the NMC 2026 guidelines?
3. Will I approve every post, caption and reply template?
4. Will all accounts, logins and assets stay in my name if we part ways?
5. Is your fee fixed, or is any part linked to enquiries or patients?
6. Do you send a crew, or do you guide my team to record on a phone?
7. What exactly will you deliver each month: how many posts, Reels and profile updates?
8. What notice period do you need if I want to stop?

## Red flags: offers to refuse

Some agencies still sell tactics that now put the doctor at risk. Refuse any of these:

- "We will get you 100 Google reviews this month."
- "We will make you No.1 on Google in your city."
- Before/after photos or patient video testimonials.
- "Free consultation camp" or discount offer ads.
- Paid ads asking patients to book with an individual doctor.
- AI-made patient stories or fake "doctor" avatars.
- Payment per lead, per call or per patient.

For what reviews can and cannot be under the new rules, read our note on [Google reviews for doctors under NMC](blog-google-reviews-for-doctors-nmc.html).

## Metro agency or local agency?

You don't need an agency in your own city. Much of the work (scripts, editing, posting, Google profile updates) happens online. What matters more:

- **Understanding your patients.** An agency that knows how patients in your region search and speak will write better content.
- **Healthcare experience.** A general agency may not know the NMC rules well.
- **Responsiveness.** You should get quick replies on WhatsApp or calls when a post needs changes.

Compare on these points, not on the size of the office or the city on the letterhead.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a healthcare-only agency, or will a general agency do?**
A general agency can do the technical work, but a healthcare-focused agency is more likely to understand the NMC 2026 rules. Ask any agency to explain those rules before you hire them.

**Is the doctor responsible if the agency posts something wrong?**
The NMC guidelines apply to the registered practitioner, so the doctor carries the risk. That is why you should approve every post.

**Can an agency still run ads for my clinic?**
Ads that solicit patients for an individual doctor are a problem under the guidelines. Hospitals may share factual details like departments, facilities and services. Ask your agency to explain what it plans before spending.

**How long before I see results?**
It depends on your starting point and city. Google profile fixes can help quickly, while content and SEO usually take a few months of steady work. Be wary of anyone who promises fixed results.

**Can an agency outside my city manage my marketing?**
Yes. Most work is done online. Check that they understand your patients' language and reply quickly.
