## What the NMC 2026 rules mean for SEO

The NMC guidelines cover websites, blogs and social media, not just hoardings. Agency summaries of the notice point to a few rules that matter directly for SEO:

- **No manipulating search rankings or reviews.** The guidelines list fake engagement, including manipulating search rankings, algorithms, reviews and ratings, as prohibited.
- **No unverifiable superlatives.** "Best", "No. 1" or "top doctor" claims are not allowed unless objectively verifiable, so page titles like "Best Gynaecologist in Varanasi" are a risk.
- **No soliciting reviews or testimonials.** A doctor should not request or share patient testimonials or reviews for promotion.
- **Fees can't be linked to patients.** Agency fees linked to bringing in individual patients are barred, so avoid pay-per-lead and pay-per-patient deals.
- **Identity details.** Posts and pages by a doctor should show the doctor's name, qualifications and registration details.

The good news: honest SEO fits these rules well. Correct details, helpful pages and a clean Google profile are patient information, not self-promotion. Our post on [Google reviews for doctors under NMC rules](blog-google-reviews-for-doctors-nmc.html) has safe reply examples.

## Local SEO checklist for Varanasi clinics

Use this to check your own setup, or any agency's plan:

1. **One verified Google Business Profile** in the clinic's real name, owned by you (not the agency).
2. **Correct category**, such as "Dentist", "Gynecologist" or "Orthopedic surgeon", not a generic "Clinic".
3. **Exact address and map pin**, with the pin code and a nearby landmark (for example, a temple, chauraha or main road).
4. **Same name, address and phone everywhere**: website, Google, listing sites, Instagram and Facebook.
5. **Timings kept current**, including Sundays and festival days like Dev Deepawali or Chhath.
6. **Real photos** of the clinic, reception and signboard, with no patients visible.
7. **Hindi and English** on key pages, because many patients search in Hinglish.
8. **Replies to reviews** that thank the patient and never mention treatment details.

## Red flags in an SEO proposal

Say no if an agency offers:

1. **Guaranteed first-page or "No. 1" rankings.** Nobody controls Google's results.
2. **Bought or "managed" reviews**, or WhatsApp messages asking patients for 5 stars.
3. **Fake locations** or extra Google profiles for clinics that don't exist.
4. **Hundreds of backlinks** from unrelated or spammy sites.
5. **Pay-per-lead or pay-per-patient fees.**
6. **Accounts in the agency's name**, so you lose your profile if you leave.
7. **Unchecked AI content** about diseases and treatments.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does SEO take for a doctor in Varanasi?**
Basic fixes, like a correct Google profile and consistent details, can be done in a few weeks. Rankings usually take months and depend on your speciality, area and competition. Be careful of anyone who promises a fixed date.

**Is SEO allowed under the NMC 2026 guidelines?**
Yes. Correct, factual information on your website and Google profile is patient information. What is not allowed is manipulating rankings or reviews, unverifiable "best" or "No. 1" claims, and asking patients for reviews or testimonials for promotion.

**Can an SEO agency guarantee the first page of Google?**
No. Google decides rankings, and they change with updates such as the November 2026 core update. An honest agency explains what it will do each month and reports real results like calls and direction requests.

**How much does an SEO agency for doctors in Varanasi cost?**
It depends on your speciality, how many pages you need, whether you need a new website, and how competitive your area is. Ask for a fixed monthly fee with a written scope, and avoid fees linked to the number of patients.

**Should my Google profile be in the agency's name?**
No. The Google Business Profile, website domain and social accounts should be owned by you or your clinic, with the agency added as a manager.
