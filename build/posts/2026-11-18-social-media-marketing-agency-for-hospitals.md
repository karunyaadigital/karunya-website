A hospital's Instagram and Facebook pages are often the first thing a family checks before they visit. Is the emergency open at night? Which departments are there? Where is the parking? That is why many hospital owners and admins now search for a **social media marketing agency for hospitals** to run these pages properly.

Since 6 October 2026, the NMC's *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* also cover how hospitals talk in public. So the agency you hire must know the rules as well as it knows Reels. This guide explains what a hospital social media agency should do, what it must avoid, and how to choose one.

## What a social media marketing agency for hospitals should do

Hospital social media is mostly about clear information and public health education. A sensible monthly scope looks like this:

- **Content plan:** a calendar of education posts, department information, health days and hospital updates.
- **Scripts and shoot guidance:** simple Hindi, Hinglish or English scripts that your doctors or staff record on a phone, with tips on light, sound and framing.
- **Editing and posting:** subtitles, thumbnails, captions and a steady posting schedule on Instagram, Facebook and YouTube.
- **Factual hospital information:** departments, facilities, equipment, emergency services, timings, contact details and accreditation, kept up to date.
- **Comment and message handling:** polite replies that guide people to call or visit, without giving personal medical advice in public.
- **Compliance check:** every post checked against the NMC guidelines before it reaches your approval.
- **Monthly report:** reach, profile visits, calls and direction requests, explained in plain words.

## What the NMC 2026 rules mean for hospital social media

Medical Dialogues summarised what hospitals may and may not publish under the new guidelines (23 October 2026).

Hospitals **may** share factual, verifiable information: name, location, contact details, departments, facilities, equipment, services, emergency services, accreditation status and applicable fees. They may also share public health education, as long as it does not turn into self-promotion.

Hospitals **may not** solicit patients, promote individual doctors, make superiority claims like "Best Doctor" or "100% Success", offer discounts or cashbacks, use paid rankings or fake reviews, use patient testimonials or clinical images for promotion, or use AI to create patient stories or outcomes. Labelling such content as AI-made does not make it allowed.

One point matters most when you hire an agency: the hospital stays responsible for content from agencies, influencers or platforms that it sponsors, adopts or knowingly allows. Outsourcing your pages does not outsource the risk.

## Allowed vs not allowed: a quick table

| Post idea | OK under NMC 2026? | Safer way to do it |
|---|---|---|
| "Our ICU and emergency are open 24x7" with contact number | Yes, if true | Keep timings and numbers current |
| Video tour of the new OPD block, no patients in frame | Yes | Show facilities, not people being treated |
| World Heart Day Reel on warning signs of a heart attack | Yes | General education, ends with "consult a doctor" |
| "Dr X is the best cardiologist in the city" | No | Factual department page listing doctors and qualifications |
| Patient thank-you video after surgery | No | Skip testimonials entirely |
| "Free health check-up this Sunday only" | No | Share education, not offers |
| Before/after images of a procedure | No | Explain the procedure in general terms |
| AI-made "patient journey" story | No | Real, general education by your doctors |
| Buying likes, followers or reviews | No | Grow slowly with useful content |

## Hospital vs clinic social media: why it is different

A clinic usually has one or two doctors who approve every post. A hospital has many departments, many doctors, an admin team and sometimes a management board. That changes the agency brief:

- **Approval flow:** decide who signs off. Often a department head checks the medical facts and an admin checks the hospital facts.
- **Doctor content:** doctors can explain conditions on the hospital page, but the post should educate, not promote that doctor over others.
- **Privacy:** wards, ICUs and waiting areas are full of patients. The agency must plan shoots so no patient, file or screen is visible.
- **Crisis replies:** complaints in comments need a calm, standard reply that never reveals patient details.

## Red flags in a hospital social media proposal

Say no if an agency offers any of these:

1. "Patient stories" or "happy patient" videos as the main content.
2. Paid ads that ask people to book a surgery or a package.
3. Monthly fees linked to the number of leads, calls or admissions.
4. Guaranteed follower numbers, or "we will make you No. 1 in the city".
5. Running the pages from the agency's own account, so you don't own them.
6. No written approval step before posting.

## Questions to ask before you sign

- Have you managed hospital pages before? Can I see them?
- Who checks each post against the NMC guidelines?
- What is the approval flow for posts made with different departments?
- Do you send a shooting crew, or guide our staff to record on phones?
- How do you keep patients out of every frame?
- Will all accounts and passwords stay in the hospital's name?
- Is the fee a fixed monthly amount?

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a hospital show its doctors on social media?**
Yes. Factual information such as a doctor's name, qualifications and department is fine, and doctors can appear in education videos. Avoid posts that promote one doctor as better than others.

**Can a hospital post its package prices?**
The guidelines allow factual information on applicable fees. Keep it accurate and avoid discount or "limited offer" language.

**Who is responsible if the agency posts something wrong?**
The hospital. The guidelines say hospitals stay responsible for content from agencies they hire or knowingly allow, which is why a written approval step matters.

**How often should a hospital post?**
Regular and steady beats daily and rushed. Agree a schedule your doctors can actually review, often a few posts and Reels a week.

**Should hospitals run paid ads on Instagram?**
Ads that solicit patients are a problem. If you use ads at all, keep them to factual information such as a new facility or emergency number, and get advice on your state's rules.
