Many patients now look up a doctor on Instagram before they book. They want to see who you are, what you treat and how you explain things. That is why so many doctors start searching for an **Instagram marketing agency for doctors**. But Instagram is also where the NMC 2026 rules bite hardest, so the agency you pick matters a lot.

This guide explains what a good Instagram agency should do for a doctor, what to ask before hiring, which offers to refuse, and how to compare costs fairly.

## What an Instagram marketing agency for doctors actually does

A doctor's Instagram is not a shop's Instagram. The goal is not "sales". The goal is clear patient education and correct information, so the right patients can find you and trust what they read.

Good agency work usually covers:

- **Planning:** a monthly content calendar based on the conditions you treat and the questions patients ask you.
- **Scripts:** short Reel scripts in simple Hindi, Hinglish or English, written so you can speak naturally.
- **Shoot guidance:** how to frame, light and record on your phone in your clinic, in 15–20 minutes.
- **Editing:** cuts, captions, subtitles and a clean, consistent look.
- **Posting:** captions, hashtags, timing and pinned posts.
- **Profile basics:** bio, highlights, contact button and links. Our [bio for doctors on Instagram](blog-bio-for-doctors-on-instagram.html) guide shows what a correct bio needs.
- **Monthly report:** reach, profile visits, saves, link taps and calls.

## The NMC 2026 rules your Instagram agency must follow

The National Medical Commission's *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* came into force on 6 October 2026. As reported by The South First, doctors may take part in health awareness and educational content, but may not use it to promote their own practice or solicit patients.

The same report notes that using third-party agencies, influencers or digital platforms will not, by itself, shield a doctor. If you authorised or knowingly allowed a post, you are responsible for it. So your agency must refuse, not suggest:

- Patient testimonial videos, "happy patient" stories or shared patient reviews. Consent alone does not make them allowed.
- Before/after photos or videos.
- Captions like "best doctor", "No.1", "guaranteed" or "100% result".
- Buying followers, likes, reviews or ratings.
- Discount posts or "free consultation" offers.
- Paid ads that ask patients to come to your clinic.
- Influencer or celebrity promotions of your practice.
- AI-made patient faces, voices or stories. The guidelines treat AI content as advertising when it is promotional.
- Pay-per-patient or pay-per-lead deals.

The South First also reports that electronic posts must show your name, qualifications, registration status and medical register number. A good agency builds this into your bio and post templates. ## Questions to ask before you hire

1. Have you managed Instagram for doctors before? Can I see those public profiles?
2. Who writes the scripts, and who checks them against the NMC rules?
3. Will every Reel and caption come to me for approval before posting?
4. Who shoots the video? Will you guide me to record on my phone, or do you send a crew?
5. Will the account stay in my name, with my email and phone number?
6. What exactly is included each month: how many Reels, posts and stories?
7. Is the fee fixed monthly? (It should be, not linked to patients or leads.)
8. How do we hand over files and access if we stop working together?

## Red flags to watch for

| What the agency says | Why it is a problem |
|---|---|
| "We will get you 10k followers in a month" | Often means bought followers; manipulation is barred |
| "Let's post patient thank-you videos" | Testimonials are not allowed, even with consent |
| "We'll run ads to bring patients" | Soliciting patients for an individual doctor is a problem |
| "Pay us per patient who comes" | Fees must not be linked to referring patients |
| "We will handle everything, no need to check" | You stay responsible for every post |
| "We'll make the account in our name" | You can lose your page if you switch agencies |

## Frequently asked questions

**Can an Instagram agency run paid ads for my clinic?**
Ads that solicit patients for an individual doctor are a problem under the NMC 2026 guidelines. Focus on patient education content and correct profile information instead.

**How many Reels should a doctor post each month?**
There is no fixed rule. A steady schedule you can keep, with each Reel checked and approved, matters more than a high number.

**Do I have to appear in the videos myself?**
It helps patients know you, but it is your choice. Educational graphics and carousels also work. A good agency writes scripts that make recording easy.

**Can my agency use AI to make videos?**
AI can help with editing or drafts, but promotional AI content counts as advertising. Never use AI-made patient faces, voices or stories.

**Should the agency have my Instagram password?**
Better not. Keep the account in your name and give the agency limited access through Meta Business Suite where possible.
