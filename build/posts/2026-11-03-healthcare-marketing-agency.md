A **healthcare marketing agency** helps doctors, clinics and hospitals get found online and explain their work clearly to patients. In 2026 that job looks different. The NMC's *Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication* (6 October 2026) have changed what is allowed, and they also make one thing clear: if an agency breaks the rules on your behalf, you are still responsible.

So before you hire anyone, it helps to know what a good agency should do, what it must never do, and what questions to ask.

## Why doctors are looking for agencies now

Most patients now check Google, Instagram or YouTube before visiting a new doctor. They look at your timings, your location, your reviews and whether you explain things clearly. A busy doctor rarely has time to manage all of that.

At the same time, the new NMC rules have made many old "doctor marketing" tricks risky. Testimonials, "best doctor" claims, paid rankings and before/after posts are out. That means doctors now need help that is both practical and rule-aware.

## What a healthcare marketing agency should do

A good agency's work falls into a few clear areas. All of them are about patient education and being easy to find.

| Area | What good work looks like |
|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Correct name, category, timings, map pin, real clinic photos, polite replies to reviews |
| Website and SEO | One clear page per service, fast mobile site, local keywords, factual information |
| Instagram and Reels | Patient-education topics, scripts, shoot guidance, editing and posting |
| YouTube | Longer explainer videos on common questions in your speciality |
| Compliance check | Every post checked against NMC rules before it goes live |
| Monthly report | Simple numbers you can understand: searches, calls, direction requests, views |

## What the NMC rules now say about agencies

This is the part many doctors miss. Reports on the guidelines say that using a digital platform, advertising agency, influencer or other intermediary does not, by itself, absolve a doctor or institution from responsibility for content they authorised, commissioned, sponsored, adopted or knowingly permitted.

In simple words: if your agency posts something illegal on your page, the NMC can still hold you accountable.

Two other points matter when you pick an agency:

- **Payment can't be linked to patients.** As reported, the guidelines say agency, influencer or platform arrangements should not be structured so that payment is linked to getting or referring individual patients. Be careful with "pay per patient" or "pay per lead" deals.
- **No doing indirectly what you can't do directly.** Doctors can't use a third party as an intermediary to carry out prohibited activity. A "page run by fans" or an influencer posting testimonials for you is still a problem.

## What a good agency will never do

If an agency offers any of these, treat it as a red flag:

- Write or post patient testimonials, or "patient story" videos
- Post before/after photos of treatment
- Use words like "best", "No.1", "guaranteed" or "100% success"
- Buy reviews, followers, likes or views, or ask patients for reviews in exchange for anything
- Run discounts, "free consultation" offers or ads that solicit patients for an individual doctor
- Create AI-made patient stories, voices or results
- Pay to push you up the rankings on doctor-listing platforms

These are not just ethical issues. Reported penalties range from a warning and ethics training for a first violation up to suspension of registration for repeat or serious violations.

## Questions to ask before you sign

Use this short checklist in your first meeting:

1. **Who checks content against NMC rules before posting?** You want a named person and a clear process.
2. **Will I approve every post?** You should. Your name and registration are on the line.
3. **How do you charge?** A fixed monthly fee for defined work is cleaner than payment tied to patient numbers.
4. **What exactly is included?** Ask for numbers: how many reels, posts, articles and profile updates per month.
5. **What will you report every month?** Look for things you can verify yourself in Google Business Profile and Instagram insights.
6. **Who owns my accounts and content?** The pages, logins and videos should stay in your name.

## Agency vs doing it yourself vs a freelancer

There is no single right answer. It depends on your time, budget and how many platforms you want to cover.

| Option | Works well when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Do it yourself | You enjoy making content and have a few hours every week | It is easy to stop after a month |
| Freelancer | You need one skill, like video editing | They may not know NMC rules |
| Healthcare marketing agency | You want Google, Instagram and website handled together | Check compliance process and contract terms |

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it legal for doctors to hire a marketing agency in India?**
Yes. Doctors can share factual, educational information and use agencies to help. The doctor stays responsible for what is published, so the agency must follow the NMC guidelines.

**Can a healthcare marketing agency run ads for my clinic?**
Ads that solicit patients for an individual doctor, offer discounts or make superiority claims are not allowed. Factual awareness content is a safer route. Check every ad idea against the guidelines before spending.

**Should I pay an agency per patient or per lead?**
Be careful. Reports say the guidelines don't allow agency arrangements where payment is linked to getting or referring individual patients. A fixed monthly fee for clear work is the simpler option.

**How long before I see results?**
Profile fixes can show changes in local searches fairly soon. Content and SEO usually build over several months. Be wary of anyone promising quick, guaranteed results.

**What should I prepare before talking to an agency?**
Your clinic details, a list of questions patients often ask, and access to your Google Business Profile and Instagram account.
