NMC penalties for doctors' advertising: warning to suspension, explained simply

Bhanu Pratap Rai7 November 2026 · 6 min read

Many doctors have read what the new rules ban. Fewer know what actually happens if a post crosses the line. This guide explains the NMC penalties for doctors' advertising under the Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication released on 6 October 2026, in plain language.

The short version: penalties are graded. A first mistake brings a warning. Repeated or serious ones can cost you your registration for months or years.

Who takes action

According to Medical Dialogues' report on the guidelines, action against an individual doctor (a registered medical practitioner, or RMP) is taken by the State Medical Council under the NMC Act and its regulations.

Hospitals and medical institutions are handled differently, under the Clinical Establishments Act and its rules, or the relevant state law. But if a doctor personally authorised, took part in or endorsed a banned hospital ad, that doctor's own conduct can also be examined.

The five penalty levels

Level What it covers Reported penalty
1 First violation Warning and mandatory ethics training
2 Second violation Censure and a monetary penalty
3 Third violation Suspension of registration for 3 to 6 months
4 Serious violation Suspension for 6 to 12 months
5 Repeated violations Removal from the medical register for 1 to 3 years

The report gives examples of a "serious" violation: misleading cure claims, inducing patients, and digital mass solicitation. These skip the gentle first steps, so they are the ones to watch most carefully.

The amount of the monetary penalty at level 2 was not stated in the report.

How the process works

A penalty is not supposed to arrive out of the blue. The guidelines, as reported, require:

  1. A show-cause notice to the doctor
  2. A chance to explain or clarify
  3. A reasoned, "speaking" order from the State Medical Council that states the specific violation, the doctor's reply, why it was accepted or rejected, and the basis for the penalty

The guidelines also allow corrective steps such as ethics sensitisation, where the law permits.

How the 60-day appeal works

On 14 October 2026, Medical Dialogues reported the appeal route set out in the guidelines:

  • First appeal: A doctor who disagrees with the State Medical Council's decision can appeal to the NMC's Ethics and Medical Registration Board (EMRB) within 60 days, under Section 30(3) of the NMC Act, 2019.
  • What the EMRB can do: call for records, order a fresh assessment, seek another opinion, and then affirm, change or set aside the council's decision in a reasoned order.
  • Second appeal: The doctor can challenge the first-appeal decision within 60 days of being told about it, under Section 30(4) of the NMC Act.

If you ever receive a notice, speak to a lawyer early. This article is general information, not legal advice.

What usually triggers trouble

Based on what the guidelines prohibit, these are the posts most likely to lead to a complaint:

  • Patient testimonials, including reposted Google reviews
  • Before-and-after photos
  • "Best", "No.1", "guaranteed" or "100% cure" claims
  • Discount offers, free-consultation camps or package deals aimed at patients
  • Bought followers, likes or reviews, directly or through an agency
  • AI-made patient stories or fake results

Remember that an agency posting for you does not take the blame for you. If it carries your name, it is your responsibility. That is why we wrote about what a healthcare marketing agency should and shouldn't do.

A 20-minute self-check for your profiles

Content posted before 6 October is still live, so it counts. Set aside 20 minutes this week:

  1. Instagram grid and Highlights: remove testimonials, before-after posts and offer posts
  2. Bio and captions: remove "best", "No.1", "guaranteed" and similar words
  3. Google Business Profile: check your business name has no extra keywords or claims, and your replies to reviews share no patient details (see our Google reviews under NMC rules guide)
  4. Website: check service pages for cure promises and success-rate claims
  5. Agency contract: make sure no one is paid per patient or per lead

For the full list of what is allowed, read our NMC new guidelines explainer.

What is still safe

The rules are not a ban on being online. Factual information like your name, qualifications, timings, address and services is fine. So is accurate patient education: explaining a condition, when to see a doctor, or what to expect from a test. That kind of content is also what patients find most useful.

Frequently asked questions

What is the penalty for a first NMC advertising violation? As reported, a first violation brings a warning and mandatory ethics training from the State Medical Council.

Can a doctor be suspended for a social media post? Yes. A third violation can lead to 3 to 6 months' suspension, and a serious violation, like misleading cure claims or mass digital solicitation, can lead to 6 to 12 months.

Who imposes the penalty, the NMC or the state council? For individual doctors, the State Medical Council takes action. Appeals go to the NMC's Ethics and Medical Registration Board.

How long do I have to appeal? 60 days from the State Medical Council's decision for the first appeal, and 60 days from the first-appeal decision for a second appeal.

Do old posts from before October 2026 count? They are still visible to patients, so it's safest to treat them as current and clean them up now.
