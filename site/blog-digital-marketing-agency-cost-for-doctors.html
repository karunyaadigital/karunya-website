Digital marketing agency cost for doctors: what you pay for in 2026

Bhanu Pratap Rai10 November 2026 · 6 min read

"How much will it cost?" is usually the first question a doctor asks an agency. It is a fair question, but the answer only makes sense once you know what you are paying for. Two quotes for the same fee can include very different amounts of work.

This guide explains digital marketing agency cost for doctors in plain terms: what drives the price, the common pricing models, the extras that get added later, and what the NMC's 2026 rules say about how an agency may be paid. It is written for doctors, clinic owners and hospital admins who are comparing quotes.

Why agency quotes for doctors vary so much

Agencies don't sell one fixed product. A quote is really a bundle of tasks, and the price moves with each one. The main things that change the cost are:

  • How many channels are covered. Google Business Profile only, or Google plus Instagram, website and YouTube.
  • How much content is made each month. Number of Reels, posts and blog articles.
  • Who does the shoot. Some agencies script and guide while you record on your phone; others send a crew, which costs more.
  • Editing quality. Simple cuts with captions cost less than motion-graphic Reels.
  • Compliance work. Checking every post against NMC (or, for dentists, NDC) rules takes time. It should be included, not skipped.
  • Number of doctors. A hospital with ten departments needs far more work than a solo clinic.

So when you compare two agencies, compare the scope line by line before you compare the fee.

Common pricing models (and which ones are safe)

Pricing model How it works Safe under NMC 2026?
Fixed monthly retainer A set fee every month for a defined scope Yes
One-time project Website build or profile setup for a fixed fee Yes
Hourly or per-task You pay per Reel, per post or per hour Yes, but costs are hard to predict
Ad spend + management fee You pay the platform; the agency charges to run ads Only if the ad content is factual and does not solicit patients for an individual doctor
Pay per patient / per lead The agency is paid for each appointment or enquiry No, avoid this

The last row matters. According to a Healthus.ai summary of the NMC Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication (6 October 2026), agency fees must not be linked to procuring or referring individual patients. A Medical Dialogues analysis (23 October 2026) also notes that hiring an agency does not remove the doctor's or hospital's responsibility for content they sponsor or knowingly permit. So a "pay only for results" offer is not a bargain if it puts your registration at risk.

What a monthly fee should include

Whatever the price, ask for the scope in writing. A clear proposal for a clinic usually lists:

Item What to ask
Google Business Profile Who updates timings, photos, categories and review replies?
Reels and posts How many per month? Who writes the scripts?
Shoot Do I record on my phone with your guidance, or do you send a crew?
Editing and posting Is captioning, thumbnails and scheduling included?
Website or SEO Is it included, or a separate project?
Compliance check Who checks each post against the NMC rules before I approve it?
Reporting What numbers will I see every month, and can I verify them myself?

If a quote is much lower than others, one of these rows is usually missing.

Hidden costs to ask about upfront

Some costs only show up after you sign. Ask about each one:

  • Ad budget. Money paid to Google or Meta is separate from the agency fee.
  • Website hosting and domain renewals.
  • Stock music, images or software licences.
  • Extra Reels beyond the monthly limit.
  • Setup or onboarding fee.
  • Exit costs. Will you keep your Google profile, Instagram account and website in your name if you leave?

Account ownership is the one we would never compromise on. Your profiles should stay in your name from day one.

Red flags that make a "cheap" quote expensive

A low fee can hide work that breaks the 2026 rules. Be careful if a proposal includes:

  • Patient testimonial videos or "patient stories"
  • Before/after photos
  • Words like "best", "No.1", "guaranteed" or "100% success" in your posts
  • Buying followers, views or Google reviews
  • Discount or free-consultation offers for patients
  • AI-made patient faces, voices or outcomes
  • Payment per patient or per lead

The Medical Dialogues analysis lists claims such as "No. 1 Hospital", "Best Doctor" and "100% Success" as examples of misleading claims, and says paid rankings and fake reviews are prohibited. An agency that offers these is offering you risk, not growth. See also our guide to Google reviews for doctors under NMC rules.

How to compare two quotes fairly

  1. Put both proposals side by side using the table above.
  2. Count the actual monthly output: Reels, posts, profile updates.
  3. Check who does the compliance check and whether you approve each post.
  4. Confirm account ownership and exit terms.
  5. Ask what the monthly report will show.
  6. Only then compare the fee.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a digital marketing agency cost for doctors in India? It depends on the scope: number of channels, monthly content, editing level and number of doctors. Compare what is included, not just the fee. Karunya Digital's plans are Rs 20,000, Rs 25,000 and Rs 40,000 per month.

Is ad spend included in the agency fee? Usually not. Money paid to Google or Meta for ads is normally separate. Ask the agency to confirm this in writing.

Is pay-per-patient marketing allowed for doctors? Summaries of the NMC's 2026 guidelines say agency payment must not be linked to procuring or referring individual patients. Choose a fixed fee or project fee instead.

Why is one agency much cheaper than another? Usually because the scope is smaller, or because compliance checks, editing or reporting are left out. Compare line by line.

Do I have to pay extra for a shoot crew? That depends on the agency. Many doctors record on their phone with a script and guidance, which keeps costs lower.
