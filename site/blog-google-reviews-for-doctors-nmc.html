Google reviews for doctors under NMC rules: what you can and can't do in 2026

Bhanu Pratap Rai3 November 2026 · 6 min read

Patients read your Google reviews before they call your clinic. That has not changed. What has changed is the rulebook. Under the NMC's Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication (6 October 2026), the way doctors handle Google reviews for doctors under NMC rules now carries real risk if it is done wrong.

This guide explains, in simple terms, what is safe, what is banned, and how to reply to reviews without breaking patient confidentiality.

What the NMC guidelines say about reviews

Reports on the guidelines are clear on a few points:

  • Doctors and hospitals must not buy or manipulate patient reviews, ratings, testimonials or endorsements.
  • Fake followers, likes, reviews, ratings, comments or views are not allowed.
  • Using a third party, such as an agency or influencer, to do this indirectly is also not allowed.
  • Using AI to fabricate patient experiences, results or endorsements is banned.

Reported penalties go from a warning and ethics training for a first violation up to suspension of registration for repeat or serious ones. So this is not something to leave to a cheap "review package".

What Google's own rules say

Google has its own policy, and it lines up with the NMC on most points. Google's help page for business owners says offering rewards, such as free or discounted services, in exchange for reviews, changed reviews or removed negative reviews is treated as fake content and is strictly prohibited.

Google Maps has also started asking some visitors directly: "Does this business offer rewards in exchange for reviews?" So incentive schemes are more likely to be spotted than before.

Safe vs risky: a quick table

Practice Safe? Why
Replying politely to every review Yes Shows you care, if you keep medical details out
Flagging a review that breaks Google's policy Yes Google allows owners to report policy violations

Paying for reviews or ratings No Banned by NMC and Google

Our advice is also not to ask patients for reviews at all. Let reviews come on their own from patients who choose to write them, and put your energy into a good clinic experience and an accurate profile.

How to reply to a positive review

Keep it short, warm and general. Never confirm that the person is your patient, and never mention a condition or treatment.

Good reply: "Thank you for taking the time to write this. Our team is glad you found the clinic easy to visit."

Avoid: "Glad your knee surgery went well and you're walking again!" This confirms a patient relationship and shares health details in public.

How to reply to a negative review

Negative reviews feel personal, but a calm reply helps every future reader.

  1. Wait a few hours before replying, so you're not writing in anger.
  2. Don't argue or share details. No dates, no diagnosis, no "our records show".
  3. Acknowledge and move offline. For example: "We're sorry to hear about your experience. Please call the clinic reception so we can understand and help."
  4. Fix what's fixable. If many reviews mention waiting time or rude staff, that is useful feedback for the clinic.

Google's own guidance for owners also says to keep replies professional, polite and short, protect privacy and avoid personal attacks.

What to do about a fake or abusive review

Sometimes a review comes from someone who was never a patient, or it contains abuse. You can flag it from your Google Business Profile as violating Google's policies. Stay factual in the report. Don't reply with accusations in public, and don't post a counter-review from friends or staff.

Where reviews fit in your bigger plan

Reviews are one part of how patients find and judge your clinic. They work best alongside:

  • An accurate Google Business Profile with correct timings and map pin
  • A simple website with clear service pages (see our SEO for doctors guide)
  • Patient-education content on Instagram or YouTube that answers common questions

Frequently asked questions

Can doctors have Google reviews under the new NMC rules? Yes. Reviews written freely by patients on Google are not banned. What is banned is buying, faking or manipulating reviews and ratings, or using them as testimonials in promotion.

Can I share a good Google review on my Instagram page? It's safer not to. Reposting patient praise to promote your practice can count as a testimonial, which the guidelines prohibit.

Can I delete bad reviews? You can't delete a genuine review yourself. You can flag a review that breaks Google's policies, such as spam or abuse, and reply politely to the rest.

Is it okay for my agency to manage review replies? Yes, if every reply follows the rules: polite, short and with no medical details. Remember that you remain responsible for what an agency posts on your behalf.

Should I offer a discount to patients who leave a review? No. Google treats rewards for reviews as fake content, and the NMC bans manipulated reviews and discount-style inducements.
