How to choose a social media agency for doctors in India

Bhanu Pratap Rai1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Most doctors today know they need to be on Instagram and Google. The harder question is who should handle it. A friend's nephew? A general digital agency? Or a social media agency that works only with doctors?

This guide explains what a good marketing agency for doctors should do, what to ask before you sign, and the red flags to avoid.

Why doctors need a specialised agency

Healthcare marketing is different from marketing a restaurant or a showroom.

  • Patients are worried, not just curious. Content has to calm fear and explain clearly, not just look attractive.
  • There are professional rules. Since 6 October 2026, the NMC's ethical advertising guidelines apply to every post. Claims like "best doctor", "guaranteed results", patient testimonials and paid reviews are not allowed, and using an agency does not shift the responsibility away from the doctor.
  • Trust is personal. Patients choose a doctor, not a logo. Your face and voice matter more than fancy graphics.

A general agency may create beautiful posts that get likes but no appointments. A healthcare-focused agency knows which questions patients actually search for.

What a good social media agency for doctors should handle

Area What it should include
Instagram reels Topic research from real patient questions, scripts, editing, captions, posting
Google Business Profile Complete profile, photos, weekly posts, review replies
Paid promotion Only where the NMC rules allow it, such as factual hospital information; no ads that solicit patients for an individual doctor
Personal branding Consistent look, bio, highlights, co-branded logo
Reporting A simple monthly report you can actually understand

7 questions to ask before you hire an agency

  1. Do you work with other doctors? Ask to see real doctor profiles they manage, not just mock-ups.
  2. Who writes the scripts? Medical content should be written carefully and approved by you.
  3. Will every post be approved by me? The answer should always be yes.
  4. How do you handle NMC guidelines? Listen for patient education, no superlatives, no testimonials or before-and-after posts, and a clear plan for the October 2026 guidelines (our NMC guide).
  5. How much of my time will it take? A good agency asks for a little shoot time and approvals, not your whole week.
  6. What will the monthly report show? Reach, followers from your city, profile visits, calls and direction requests.
  7. Can you show me real results? Ask for real numbers from doctors they already work with: reel views, Google calls and direction requests.

Red flags to avoid

  • Promises of "10,000 followers in a month" or "number one on Google"
  • Buying followers or fake reviews
  • Copy-paste posts with stock photos that look like every other clinic
  • No clear process for your approval
  • Long contracts with no monthly reporting

Social media is a team game

No agency can build a doctor's personal brand alone. The best results come when the doctor and the team work together: the doctor shares real patient questions and gives a little time on camera, and the agency handles research, scripts, editing, posting and reporting. Patients can feel the difference when content genuinely comes from their doctor.
